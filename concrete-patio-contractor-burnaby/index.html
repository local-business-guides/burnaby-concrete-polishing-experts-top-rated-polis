Key facts about scope, timing, and cost

For a polishing conversation, these are the main points to clarify:

  • What it covers: polishing an existing concrete surface, if its condition and intended use make that suitable. Patio construction or structural repairs are separate questions to confirm.
  • Typical timing: there is no reliable set duration without details about the surface and requested work. Ask for a timing estimate after the concrete has been assessed.
  • What affects cost: the size and condition of the area, the preparation needed, and the finish requested can affect the scope. Ask for a project-specific explanation rather than relying on a general figure.
  • What to compare: ask providers to describe their process, preparation, limitations, and proposed finish in clear terms.

A few terms may come up. Polishing means mechanically working the concrete surface to create a chosen finish. Surface preparation is the work needed before that process begins. Existing slab means the concrete already in place. These terms help you understand the discussion, but they do not replace an assessment of your patio.
